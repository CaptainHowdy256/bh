The game is very simple. 

You begin by launching the Graphical Object Interface Visual Display+ (extreme edition) screen.  The left side of the screen displays a not very recent picture of a celebrity, politician, Playboy centerfold, porn star, or other easily recognizable person.  The right side of the screen displays the Arse Gallery--a collection of photographs of this same person's butt AND several other well-known butts.  Your job is match the head with the butt.  

One of the butts, and only one of the butts, matches the head.  There are no trick questions.  

Each time you correctly match the head with the butt, you get one point (called a "butthead").  Each time you screw up and pick the wrong butt, you also get a point except it's not called a "butthead."  It doesn't have a name.  You can call it whatever you want but make sure it sounds like a bad thing because that's what it is.   The goal of the game is to get all your buttheads with a minimum of that other type of point. 

The game does not keep score for you.  There are java scripts and applets that would allow the website to keep track of wrong answers and give you a numerical score at the end of the game but they would slow down the game play.  Also, they might open your computer up to hackers and their terrorist fellow-travelers.  

That's the official story.  The truth is that we don't know how to write a script or applet that would keep score.  Our V.P. of Development was going to figure it out because it would be totally cool and it's the kind of thing that he should know how to do anyway.  But the learning curve would inevitably add even more development time onto this game.    

Not that this was an issue.  The Foundation that commissioned this work did not specify a completion date.  However, after about a year of meandering through this project, working on bits and pieces whenever we had some downtime, we had an epiphany.  We read a story about Mt. Rushmore.  

It seems that in the Black Hills of South Dakota in 1927, the Mt. Rushmore project was formally dedicated.  In 1930, they had finished George Washington's head.  We'll do the math for you.  That's three years later!  Three years!!  We'd been dicking around for more than a year on this game and weren't anywhere close to completion.  If somebody can take a sixty foot high chunk of a mountain and chisel away at it until it resembles anything at all let alone the head of the first President of the United States in three years and we can't finish a stupid web site in less than two, we're losers.  Oh, sure..... the guy carving Rushmore had a cool million in Federal funds and dynamite but still....three years!!

So we were suddenly motivated.  We had a goal to beat and a deadline to meet.  Our motto became "Faster than Washington's Head."  We hurried it up and got the game done.  It's not what was originally envisioned but so what?  Mt. Rushmore was suppose to show all four presidents down to their waist and Jefferson's head was suppose to be on the right side of Washington's head before they ran out of rock and moved him over to the left side.  If they can cut corners and change plans, so can we.

So now you know the rules and the history of the game.   

If you're ready to play, we'll give you a little head to get you started.
